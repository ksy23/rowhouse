The morning triage

Kevin leads a support team of six. Each morning he sorts the overnight cases: who takes what, which are urgent, which customers need a call back. He wants it on his Home page, above everything else, without opening each case.

The goal

What you will build

You need

The Rowhouse Grid User permission set, permission to customize the application and to edit the Home page, and some cases. Our org has a few dozen, a dozen of them open.

Skip the build

To go straight to using the grid, the button below builds Service Triage as steps 1 to 4 leave it, in a few seconds, while a spinner says which grid it is on. Then go on to step 5, which puts it on the Home page. The build steps are still the way to learn how a grid is made.

1. The Grid Configuration

This grid shares the Home page with other components, so it gets a fixed Height.

  1. Open the records. In Setup, open Custom Metadata Types, and beside Grid Configuration click Manage Records, then New.
  2. Fill in:
    FieldValue
    LabelService Triage
    Grid Configuration NameService_Triage fills in by itself
    Object API NameCase
    Default Sort FieldCreatedDate
    Default Sort DirectionDescending newest first
    Height (px)480
  3. Save.

4. A Log a Call action

Log a Call is a global quick action that every org has. From a case's row it opens Salesforce's Log a Call window, and the call it saves goes on that case's activity timeline, marked completed.

  1. Open Grid Action records. In Custom Metadata Types, beside Grid Action, click Manage Records, then New.
  2. Fill in:
    FieldValue
    LabelLog a call what the menu says
    Grid Action NameTriage_Log_Call
    Grid ConfigurationService Triage
    Action TypeQuick action
    Action NameLogACall
    Show InRow menu
    Sort Order10
  3. Save.
Build it · admin

5. Put it on the Home page

  1. Edit the Home page. Open the Home tab, click the gear icon at the top right, and choose Edit Page.
  2. Add the grid at the top. Drag Rowhouse Grid from the Custom - Managed components to the top of the page's main region, and enter Service_Triage in Grid Configuration.
  3. Save and activate. Click Save. If you are asked to activate the page, click Activate, choose Org Default, and follow the prompts to assign it for desktop; then click Back (the arrow at the top left) to leave the builder.
Changing the Home page changes it for everyone

The org default Home page is what everyone sees, unless an app or profile has its own. In a production org, consider assigning the page to the support team's app or profile instead.

Use it · Kevin

6. Triage the queue

  1. Open Home.
    The Service Triage grid at the top, 480 pixels tall, newest cases first, with the rest of the Home page below it. New cases are tinted amber, with an amber bar at their left edge, High priorities are red, and escalated cases are bold.
  2. See the queue by status. Open the ▾ menu on the Status header and choose Group by Status. Each status becomes a group with its count.
    The groups follow the order of the Status picklist, as in a Salesforce report: Status: New at the top, then Working, Escalated and Closed.
  3. Take three new cases. In the New group, select the checkbox at the start of three cases. A bar floats over the top of the grid: "3 selected", with Update field and Clear.
  4. Move them to Working. Click Update field. Choose Status, choose Working, and click Apply.
    The Status cells of the selected cases turn yellow with "Working", their amber tint and bar go (rules follow your unsaved values), and the Save bar says "3 unsaved changes". Nothing is saved yet.
  5. Raise one priority. Click the Priority of one of those cases, press Enter, choose High and press Enter.
  6. Save. Click Save on the Save bar.
    The cases move to the Working group, and the raised priority turns red. The selection stays ("3 selected"), in case you want to do more to the same cases.
  7. Clear up. Click Clear in the selection bar, then open the Status menu again and choose Stop grouping by Status.
Use it · Kevin

7. Log a call from the row

  1. Run the action. On any case, click the ▾ at the right end of its row and choose Log a call.
    Salesforce's Log a Call window opens over the grid, with the case in Related To.
  2. Save it. Click Save.
    The window closes and you are back on Home.
  3. Check it. Click the case's number to open the case (Cmd-click or Ctrl-click opens it in a new browser tab). The call is on its activity timeline, marked completed.
Use it · Kevin

8. Add a case of his own

Three of the overnight cases are the same fault. Kevin opens one case to track it, without leaving Home.

  1. Add a row. Click New above the grid, beside Export.
    A row at the top of the grid, with New where a row number goes and the amber tint and bar of a new case. Salesforce's defaults for a new case are filled in: Status New, Priority Medium, and you as Owner. The Save bar says 1 new record.
  2. Describe it. Click the new row's Subject cell, type Generators trip on start-up after the September update and press Enter.
  3. Make it urgent. Click the new row's Priority, press Enter, choose High and press Enter.
  4. Save. Click Save on the Save bar.
    The case gets its number and opens the list, as the newest case should: red for High priority, with the amber tint and bar of a new case, opened a moment ago. The title counts one more item.

What you learned

Height

A fixed height lets a grid share a page with other components.

{{ic:palette}}

Row rules

A tinted row with a bar, or bold across it, from a field that is not even a column.

{{ic:check}}

Update field

One value onto many records, reviewed as unsaved edits before Save.

{{ic:bolt}}

Actions

A quick action in every row's menu, with no code.

{{ic:plus}}

New

A new record typed straight into the grid, saved with any other changes.
