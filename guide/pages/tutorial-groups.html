A group of companies

Theresa looks after customers that are groups: a parent company and the companies it owns. In Salesforce each one is its own account, and nothing shows the group at a glance. Here, a grid lists only the parent companies, and each one opens to show its subsidiaries, with their count and revenue on the parent's row.

The goal

What you will build

Two grids of accounts, built on the Grid Admin tab. Account Groups lists the accounts that have no parent. Subsidiaries appears as a tab under each of its rows, showing the accounts whose Parent Account is that row. Account looks up to itself, so one object makes both levels.

Skip the build

To go straight to using the grid, the button below builds Subsidiaries and Account Groups as steps 1 to 4 leave them, in a few seconds, while a spinner says which grid it is on. Then go on to step 5, which puts the group grid on a page. The build steps are still the way to learn how a grid is made.

Build it · admin

1. The subsidiaries grid

Build the grid that goes in the tab first, so you can choose it in step 3. You need the Rowhouse Grid Admin permission set, and Customize Application to save (see Give people access).

  1. Open the Grid Admin tab. Click the App Launcher, type Rowhouse, click the Rowhouse app, then the Grid Admin tab, and click New grid.
  2. Fill in the Grid tab:
    FieldValue
    Grid TitleSubsidiaries
    API nameSubsidiaries fills in by itself
    ObjectAccount (Account)
    Sort byAccount Name (Name)
  3. Add the columns. Click Columns (1). Account Name (Name) is already there. For each row below, click Add column, choose the field, and set Edit and Subtotal as the table says:
    FieldEditSubtotal
    Industry (Industry)✓—
    Annual Revenue (AnnualRevenue)✓Sum
    Employees (NumberOfEmployees)✓Sum
    A Subtotal gives the grid a Total row: under each group, the subsidiaries' revenue and staff added up.
  4. Save. Click Save. After a few seconds the editor reads the grid back: Columns (4).

2. The group grid

  1. Start another grid. Click Grids at the top of the Grid Admin tab, then New grid, and fill in the Grid tab:
    Grid TitleAccount Groups
    API nameAccount_Groups fills in by itself
    ObjectAccount (Account)
    Sort byAccount Name (Name)
  2. Add the columns. Click Columns (1); Account Name (Name) is already there. Add these, as in step 1:
    FieldEditSubtotal
    Parent Account ID (ParentId)✓—
    Industry (Industry)✓—
    Annual Revenue (AnnualRevenue)✓Sum
    The Parent Account ID column's Header box suggests Parent Account, in gray: a lookup is headed by what it points to.
  3. Show only the parent companies. Click Filters (0), then Add filter:
    NameTop-level accounts
    FieldParent Account ID (ParentId)
    ConditionIs blank
    A subsidiary has a parent, so it leaves this list and appears under its parent instead.
  4. Compare revenue at a glance. Click Display rules (0), then Add display rule:
    MeaningRevenue
    FieldAnnual Revenue (AnnualRevenue)
    ConditionIs not blank
    LookData bar
    A data bar fills each cell in proportion to the largest value in the column, so the biggest companies stand out.
Build it · admin

3. Link them

  1. Add the tab. Click Child tables (0), then Add child table, and fill in the row:
    GridSubsidiaries (Subsidiaries)
    Linked byParent Account ID (ParentId) already chosen
    Tab labelleave it empty: the tab is called Subsidiaries, after the grid
    Linked by is the field on the subsidiary that points to the row: its Parent Account. The list offers every lookup of Account to Account. Master Record ID is there too, last: only a merge fills it in, so it is not the one you want.
  2. Save. Click Save. The editor reads the grid back: Columns (4), Display rules (1), Filters (1), Child tables (1).
Build it · admin

4. Totals on the parent row

A child total column works out something about each row's tab: how many records it has, or the sum, average, lowest or highest of one of their fields. It can be added once the child table is saved.

  1. Count the subsidiaries. Click Columns (4), then Add child total column. In the new row:
    Field column: the list that says Choose a child tableSubsidiaries (Account_Groups_Subsidiaries)
    The list under it (what it works out)Count already chosen
    Headerleave it empty: the column is headed Subsidiaries
  2. Add up their revenue. Click Add child total column again:
    Field column: the list that says Choose a child tableSubsidiaries (Account_Groups_Subsidiaries)
    The list under itSum
    The list beside it, Choose a fieldAnnual Revenue (AnnualRevenue)
    HeaderSubsidiaries' revenue
  3. Save. Click Save: Columns (6).
Build it · admin

5. The page

  1. Create an App page. In Setup, type Lightning App Builder in the Quick Find box and click Lightning App Builder. Click New, choose App Page, click Next, enter the Label Account Groups, click Next, choose One Region, and click Done.
  2. Add the grid. Drag Rowhouse Grid from the Custom components onto the page, and enter Account_Groups in Grid Configuration. Only the group grid is placed; its tab comes with it.
  3. Save and activate. Click Save, then Activate. On the Lightning Experience tab, choose Sales, click Add page to app, then Save.

6. Open the grid

  1. Open it. In the Sales app, click Account Groups in the navigation bar (or under More).
    Every account is listed, 13 in a new Developer Edition org: none has a parent yet. Under the title, a locked chip Parent Account is blank. Data bars behind the revenue, the longest for United Oil & Gas Corp. The two subsidiaries in the sample records, United Oil & Gas, UK and United Oil & Gas, Singapore, are listed on their own, with no revenue. No row has a toggle yet, and the Subsidiaries column says 0 on every row.
Use it · Theresa

7. Join a group

The UK and Singapore companies belong to United Oil & Gas Corp. Theresa links them to it, here in the grid.

  1. Give the UK company its parent. On the United Oil & Gas, UK row, double-click the Parent Account cell and type United Oil. When the matches appear, arrow to United Oil & Gas Corp. and press Enter.
    The cell says United Oil & Gas Corp., shaded yellow as an unsaved change.
  2. And the Singapore company. Do the same on the United Oil & Gas, Singapore row.
  3. Save. Click Save in the bar at the bottom.
    "Saved 2 records." Both rows stay where they are, so you can see what you did; the Total row already counts 11 records.
  4. Refresh. Click Refresh, the round arrow above the grid.
    11 items: the two subsidiaries have left the list, because they now have a parent. United Oil & Gas Corp. has a toggle with 2 beside it, and its Subsidiaries column says 2.
Use it · Theresa

8. Fill in a subsidiary

  1. Open the group. Click the toggle beside United Oil & Gas Corp.
    A panel opens under the row with one tab, Subsidiaries (2): the Singapore and UK companies, with their Industry and Employees, and a Total row.
  2. Add their revenue. In the tab, click the Annual Revenue cell of United Oil & Gas, UK and type 1200000000; press Enter. Then the one of United Oil & Gas, Singapore: 410000000, and Enter.
    The tab's own Save bar appears inside the panel, and its Total row already says Sum $1,610,000,000, in yellow until it is saved.
  3. Save the tab. Click Save in the panel's bar.
  4. Refresh the group grid. Click Refresh above the grid.
    United Oil & Gas Corp.'s Subsidiaries' revenue says $1,610,000,000. Totals on a parent row are worked out when the grid reads its rows, so a change saved in a tab shows there after Refresh.

Data bars

Compare numbers down a column without reading them.
